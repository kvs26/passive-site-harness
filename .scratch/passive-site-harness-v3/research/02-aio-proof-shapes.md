# AIO-Proof Site/Tool Shapes — What Still Earns Google Clicks in 2026

**Research date:** 31 August 2026
**Question:** Which query/tool shapes still earn Google organic clicks despite AI Overviews (AIO) and AI Mode — and which are dead — for a solo builder of small static niche tool sites (calculators, lookups, curated-data tools) monetized via AdSense + affiliate?
**Method:** Primary/high-trust sources only (Google Search Central docs, Pew Research browsing-panel data, Ahrefs 146M-SERP + 300K-keyword studies, Seer Interactive 5.47M-query study, Semrush 10M-keyword study, Authoritas publisher report). Every number is cited. "Unknown" written where no source exists.

---

## 1. Headline CTR evidence (2025–2026)

### How much does an AIO cost you in clicks?

| Study | Finding | Method notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ahrefs (Apr 2025) | AIO presence correlates with **–34.5% CTR for position 1** (Mar 2024 vs Mar 2025, 300K keywords, GSC data) | https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks/ |
| Ahrefs (Feb 2026 update) | Now **–58% at position 1** (Dec 2023 vs Dec 2025, same 300K-keyword method). Decline persists down the SERP: pos 2 –50.8%, pos 5 –32.6%, pos 10 –19.4% | https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/ |
| Pew Research (Jul 2025) | Real-user panel (900 US adults, Mar 2025): users clicked a result on **8% of visits with an AI summary vs 15% without** (~½ the clicks); clicked a link *inside* the AIO on only **1%** of such visits; **26%** ended the browsing session entirely on AIO pages vs 16% without | https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/ |
| Seer Interactive v1 (Feb 2025) | Informational queries with AIO: organic CTR fell **1.41% → 0.64%** YoY; CTR *increased* on non-AIO queries | https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/ctr-aio |
| Seer Interactive v3 (Apr 2026; 53 brands, 5.47M queries, 2.43B impressions) | Per 1M informational impressions: **No AIO ≈ 33,500 clicks; AIO + cited ≈ 20,743; AIO + not cited ≈ 9,445**. Being cited = +120% vs not cited, but still **–38% vs no AIO**. Uncited organic CTR on AIO SERPs fell **–67% over 2025**. BUT: early-2026 partial rebound — AIO-present organic CTR climbed from 1.3% (Dec 2025) to 2.4% (Feb 2026); no-AIO CTR rose to 3.8% | https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update |
| Authoritas via Press Gazette (Jul 2025) | UK publishers: **–47.5% CTR on desktop, –37.7% on mobile** when AIO present; report judges it "feasible that Google could return AI Overviews for more than 90% of queries if it chose to" | https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/google-ai-overviews-publishers-report-clickthroughs-authoritas-report/ |

**Countervailing datapoints (honesty check):**
- Semrush/Datos (Dec 2025, 200K+ keywords with clickstream): for the *same keywords* before/after gaining an AIO, zero-click rate actually **decreased 33.75% → 31.53%**, and overall zero-click rate of AIO keywords declined through 2025 — AIO-prone queries were low-click *before* AIO existed. https://www.semrush.com/blog/semrush-ai-overviews-study/
- Seer v1 made the same point: AIO-eventual queries had lower CTR *pre-AIO* (selection effect, like featured snippets). https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/ctr-aio
- Seer v3: **non-AIO informational queries' CTR rose 2.93% → 3.97% across 901M impressions in 2025** — clicks are concentrating on SERPs Google chooses not to summarize. https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update
- Google's official position: AIOs "are only shown when our systems determine that it is additive… and as such, often don't trigger"; clicks from AIO SERPs are "higher quality." No special markup exists or is needed to appear/not appear. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features

### Which query categories trigger AIO most/least

**Ahrefs, 146M SERPs, Sept 2025** (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-triggers/):
- Baseline: **21% of all keywords** trigger an AIO.
- By intent: Informational **21.4%**, Commercial **4.3%**, Transactional **2.1%**, Navigational **0.9%**. 99.9% of AIO keywords are informational.
- By Google query-classifier type: **reason 59.8%, bool (yes/no) 57.4%, definition 47.3%, consequence 46.7%, short_fact 41.2%, instruction 35.1%, comparison 26.2%, other 7.6%**.
- Questions: **57.9%** of all question queries trigger AIO. Non-question: 15.5%.
- Length: 1-word 9.5% → 7+ words **46.4%**.
- "Do" intent queries (user trying to accomplish a task — the intent class of tool/calculator queries): **13%**, below baseline. "Website" queries: 0.9%. Local: 7.9%.
- Category extremes: Science 43.6%, Health 43.0%, **Pets & Animals 36.8%**, Reference 30.1% … Shopping 3.2%, Real Estate 5.8%.
- Medical YMYL: 44.1% — Google is *not* shy about YMYL AIOs.

**Pew, Mar 2025 real-user data** (https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/): 18% of all searches produced an AIO; 1–2-word searches → 8% AIO; 10+ words → 53%; question-word queries → 60%.

**Seer v3, Feb 2026 snapshot, 49,353 queries** (https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update): Informational intent SERPs show AIO **36%** of the time vs Commercial 8% and Transactional 5%. By query format: **Comparison "X vs Y" 95.4%**, Review 86.3%, **Question 85.9%**, Price/Cost/Buy 83.4%, Best-of 81.3%, Near-me 76.9%; short queries not safe (single-word 27.3%).

**Semrush, 10M+ keywords, Dec 2025** (https://www.semrush.com/blog/semrush-ai-overviews-study/): AIO share of all queries: 6.49% (Jan 2025) → 24.61% (Jul) → 15.69% (Nov) — volatile, not monotonic. **Intent mix is broadening**: informational share of AIO queries fell 91.3% → 57.1% (Jan→Oct 2025); transactional keywords triggering AIO rose 1.98% → 13.94%; navigational 0.84% → 10.33%. (Note: Semrush and Ahrefs disagree on transactional/navigational rates — different keyword sets, classifiers, and dates. Treat "non-informational is safe" as *currently true but eroding*.)

**Tool/calculator queries specifically:** No published study isolates "calculator"/"tool" queries' AIO trigger rate — **unknown** as a direct stat. Best proxies: Ahrefs "Do" intent = 13% AIO (below the 20.5% baseline); short non-question queries ("X calculator") = 9.5–15.5%. Note Google itself classifies Calculator and Unit Converter as *its own SERP features* under informational intent (Seer v3 methodology appendix, https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update) — for simple math/conversions the threat is Google's built-in widget, not the AIO.

**AI Mode:** Google says AI Mode targets "queries where further exploration, reasoning, or complex comparisons are needed" and uses "query fan-out" — multiple sub-searches — to answer complex multi-constraint questions (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features). Authoritas: "Google is using generative AI to answer almost every user question and follow-up question in AI Mode" (https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/google-ai-overviews-publishers-report-clickthroughs-authoritas-report/). No large-scale AI Mode CTR study exists yet — **unknown** magnitude, direction clearly negative for informational content.

## 2. Taxonomy of shapes, ranked most → least AIO-resistant

Scoring logic: (i) does the head query trigger AIO? (ii) can a text summary *fully* satisfy the user? (iii) is there a post-SERP action (input, download, filter, repeat use) that a summary can't replace?

### Rank 1 — (a) Interactive calculator needing user-specific input — RESISTANT
- **Does AIO trigger?** For tool-form queries ("X calculator", 2–3 words, not a question): low probability — "Do" intent 13%, non-question 15.5%, 2-word 9.9% (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-triggers/). For question-form equivalents ("how much X for a Y-lb Z"): high — questions 57.9–85.9% (Ahrefs triggers study; Seer v3).
- **Can AIO fully answer?** No, when the answer genuinely depends on multiple user-specific inputs. AIO returns a generic rule-of-thumb or a hedge; it cannot run the user's numbers. (Inference from AIO's design as a text synthesis of web consensus — Google: AIOs "help people get to the gist of a complicated topic," https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features. No study directly measures calculator-query CTR — **unknown** as a stat.)
- **Do users still click?** Structurally yes for the tool query; the question-form long-tail is partially lost to AIO's generic answer. Risk: AI Mode can *build* an inline calculator-style answer for simple formulas; multi-input, data-backed calculators (needing a brand/product lookup table) are harder to replicate.
- **Caveat:** the calculation must not be one Google widgetizes (see shape g).

### Rank 2 — (d) Printable/downloadable artifact generator — RESISTANT
- **Does AIO trigger?** "Printable X" / "X template PDF" queries are Do-intent, short, non-question → low trigger probability (Ahrefs: Do 13%, non-question 15.5%). No shape-specific study — **unknown** as a direct stat.
- **Can AIO fully answer?** No. AIO/AI Mode produce text on the SERP; they do not deliver a file, a formatted PDF, or a print layout. The user's goal *requires* leaving the SERP.
- **Do users still click?** Yes by necessity. Residual risk is not AIO but chatbots (ChatGPT can generate simple artifacts) and Google's own template features — **unknown** magnitude, no study found.

### Rank 3 — (b) Model-number/SKU lookup against a curated database — RESISTANT
- **Does AIO trigger?** SKU/model-number queries are entity-specific, quasi-navigational, low-volume. Branded queries trigger AIO at 13.1% vs 24.9% non-branded; navigational 0.9% (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-triggers/). No SKU-specific study — **unknown** as a direct stat.
- **Can AIO fully answer?** Sometimes, and dangerously: LLMs hallucinate specs for obscure SKUs, but when the AIO does answer confidently ("filter for fridge model X = part Y") the click is lost. AIO fully citing 3+ sources 88% of the time (Pew) means fragmented attribution even when cited.
- **Do users still click?** Purchase-adjacent lookups retain clicks (Shopping category AIO rate: 3.2%, Ahrefs triggers study; Shopping among lowest-AIO industries, Semrush). Watch item: Semrush shows navigational AIO share rising fast (0.84%→10.33% over 2025), so this shape's moat is real but eroding at the edges (https://www.semrush.com/blog/semrush-ai-overviews-study/).

### Rank 4 — (c) Multi-constraint filter/finder over a dataset — MIXED, TRENDING WORSE
- **Does AIO trigger?** Multi-constraint queries are long (5–7+ words → 27.6–46.4% AIO, Ahrefs triggers) and often phrased as questions/comparisons (comparison format: 95.4% AIO per Seer v3; comparison classifier: 26.2% per Ahrefs — the discrepancy is format "X vs Y" vs broader classifier).
- **Can AIO fully answer?** Partially today; this is *exactly* what AI Mode is built for — Google: AI Mode handles "complex comparisons… that might have previously taken multiple searches" via query fan-out (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).
- **Do users still click?** Yes when the dataset is proprietary/curated and the constraint space is large (AI Mode fans out to *websites* — a unique dataset can be the cited source). But head "best X for Y" queries are heavily AIO'd (Best-of: 81.3%, Seer v3). Survives only on dataset uniqueness + interactive filtering UX, not on the query itself.

### Rank 5 — (e) Static data table / reference page — WEAK
- **Does AIO trigger?** Reference category: 30.1% AIO; short_fact classifier: 41.2%; definition: 47.3% (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-triggers/).
- **Can AIO fully answer?** For any *single* value in the table, yes — AIO extracts it. The full-table view (comparison shopping across many rows) still needs the page.
- **Do users still click?** Diminished: this is the featured-snippet dynamic amplified; AIO keywords trigger 849% more featured snippets than non-AIO (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-keywords/). Only survives if the table is large, dense, sortable — i.e., if it behaves like shape (c).

### Rank 6 — (g) Simple unit converter — DEAD (but not because of AIO)
- Google serves its **own Calculator and Unit Converter SERP widgets** — listed as Google SERP features in Seer's intent-classification appendix (https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update). The query is fully answered on-SERP by a deterministic widget, pre-dating AIO. No CTR study specific to converter queries found — **unknown** as a stat, but the mechanism requires no study: the widget computes the exact answer.
- Only conversions Google does *not* widgetize (obscure domain-specific units, e.g., washer drum volume → laundry-load counts) escape this — those are really shape (a).

### Rank 7 — (f) Plain informational article — DEAD for click-dependent monetization
- Questions: 57.9% AIO (Ahrefs triggers) to 85.9% (Seer v3); reason queries 59.8%; how-to/instruction 35.1%; comparison format 95.4% (Seer v3).
- Position-1 CTR down **58%** where AIO present (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/); users click AIO citation links on only **1% of visits** (Pew).
- 99.2–99.9% of AIO-triggering keywords are informational (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-keywords/, https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-triggers/). Ahrefs' verdict on the adjacent business model: "Affiliate marketing is harder than ever… The window to easy profit has closed" (https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-is-still-your-best-marketing-channel/).
- Narrow exception: informational queries where Google chooses *not* to show an AIO saw CTR **rise** 2.93%→3.97% in 2025 (Seer v3) — worth targeting, but you can't control Google's trigger decision.

## 3. Scout-time tests (cheap, observable, no paid APIs)

**Constraint:** the agent's default SERP tool is DuckDuckGo, which has no Google AIO — DDG results tell you *nothing* about AIO presence. Options for observing Google AIO behavior for free:

1. **Manual Google check (primary method).** A human (or the agent's integrated browser, human-in-the-loop) loads `https://www.google.com/search?q=<query>` in a regular browser and records: (i) AIO present? (ii) does it state a complete, specific answer, or hedge ("consult your vet", "varies by…") and link out? (iii) is a Google widget (calculator/unit converter/shopping pack) present? Note: automated scraping of Google SERPs violates Google's ToS; keep this as a manual spot-check of ~3–5 head queries per idea, not a crawl.
2. **`&udm=14` as the control arm.** Appending `udm=14` shows Google's "Web" filter — results with no AIO and no SERP features (announced by Google Search Liaison Danny Sullivan, May 2024; documented at https://tedium.co/2024/05/17/google-web-search-make-default/ and https://udm14.com/). Compare default SERP vs `udm=14` SERP: if your target page type ranks well in `udm=14` but the default SERP buries it under an AIO that fully answers, the shape is compromised. (udm=14 *removes* the AIO — it's the clean-ranking view, not an AIO detector.)
3. **Predict-from-shape heuristic (zero SERP access).** Use published trigger rates as priors before any manual check: question word present → ~58–86% AIO; "vs"/comparison → ~95%; yes/no ("can/is/does…") → ~57%; 7+ words → ~46%; 1–2 word tool-noun query → ~9–10%; Do-intent phrasing → ~13%; Pets & Animals topic → ~37% baseline. (Sources: Ahrefs triggers study; Seer v3; Pew — all cited in §1.)
4. **Free tool-based checks:** Ahrefs/Semrush AIO filters require paid plans. Free-tier AIO checkers: **unknown/unverified** — do not rely on them.

**Per-shape scout tests:**
- **(a) Calculator:** search the tool query ("dog treat calculator") AND the question form ("how many treats for a 20 lb dog"). Pass = tool query has no AIO (or AIO links to calculators), and question-form AIO gives only a generic rule/hedge, not a number for arbitrary inputs. Fail = AIO states a usable specific answer for the most common input.
- **(b) SKU lookup:** search 3 real model numbers from the niche. Pass = no AIO, or AIO hedges/errs on specifics (verify against manufacturer data). Also record who ranks — if it's all manufacturer PDFs and forum threads, the curated-DB gap is real.
- **(c) Finder:** search the natural multi-constraint phrasing ("quietest 24 inch dishwasher under $700 with third rack"). Pass = AIO/AI Mode gives generic guidance and links out; fail = it returns a confident short list that satisfies. Also check the "best X for Y" head term — expect AIO (~81%, Seer v3); the shape survives on the interactive filter, not the head term.
- **(d) Generator:** search "printable X" / "X template". Pass = no AIO or AIO describes-but-can't-deliver; confirm SERP is file-delivering sites.
- **(e) Table:** search 2 single-value queries answerable from the table. If AIO answers each value, the page only survives as a sortable/filterable tool → reclassify as (c).
- **(g) Converter:** search the conversion. If Google's widget computes it → dead, skip. No AIO check needed.
- **Universal kill-check:** run the niche's top head query; if the AIO answer is complete, specific, and correct, and the Pew 1%-citation-click reality applies, assume ≤ half of historical position-1 clicks (Ahrefs –58%) in the plan's traffic model.

## 4. Sanity check against two live ideas

**Context:** Pets & Animals triggers AIO at **36.8%** — 4th-highest category of 25 (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-triggers/). Both ideas start in a high-AIO neighborhood.

### Dog-treat portion calculator (dog weight + treat brand → portion) — SURVIVES, with positioning discipline
- Shape (a), the most resistant class. The answer depends on two user inputs (weight × brand calorie data), and brand kcal data is a curated-DB component (shape b hybrid) that AIO can hallucinate but not reliably compute against.
- The question-form head queries ("how many treats can I give my dog") are question+pets → very likely AIO'd (57.9% question baseline, 36.8% category; Seer v3 question format 85.9%), and the AIO will give the generic "10% rule" — a partial answer that hedges on brand specifics. Per taxonomy that's a *hedge, not a full answer*: brand-specific, weight-specific output is the click-worthy residual.
- Plan for: tool-form query targeting ("dog treat calculator", "[brand] treat calories"), brand long-tail pages, and accepting that generic question traffic is mostly gone. Verify at scout time with the §3(a) test.

### "Can my dog eat X" food-safety database — FAILS the taxonomy as a plain database
- Shape (e)/(f) hybrid on bool queries: yes/no queries trigger AIO **57.4%** (Ahrefs triggers); question format 85.9% (Seer v3); Pets & Animals 36.8%. Ahrefs literally uses **"can dogs have cinnamon"** as its example AIO keyword (KD 12, AIO present) — https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-keywords/.
- AIO *fully* answers the head query ("yes, in small amounts…") — a complete, satisfying, low-stakes-feeling answer. Pew: only 1% of AIO-page visits click a summary link. This is the canonical dead shape.
- Survivable pivot: convert the yes/no lookup into shape (a) — quantity/toxicity-by-weight calculators ("my 18-lb dog ate 40 g of dark chocolate — is that dangerous?"), where the answer is input-specific and the AIO can only hedge toward "call your vet." That's an emergency-utility calculator, not a database. (Consistent with the existing chocolate-toxicity-calculator idea in the pipeline.)
