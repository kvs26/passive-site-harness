# Niche: occasion-poem-finder

Tools for people who need **the right poem for a life event** — funerals / celebrations of life, weddings, anniversaries, memorials, sympathy cards, retirements — and who then read it aloud or print it on a program or card. Audience is US grieving families, celebrants/officiants, wedding couples, and card/program makers.

**Target market: US.** No override.

## Seeds

- funeral poems for / poems to read at a funeral
- wedding poems for / poem to read at a wedding
- short / non-religious / secular poem for [occasion]
- public-domain poem for [occasion] / copyright to print a poem
- celebration of life / eulogy readings

## Known incumbents (as of 2026-08-29)

**Occasion listicles / relationship finders (SATURATED):** familyfriendpoems.com, griefandsympathy.com ("Funeral Poems & Readings: By Relationship, Occasion & Style"), funeralguide, poetryfoundation.org collections, poets.org "poems for every occasion", poemhunter, allpoetry, eulogyexpert.com, tributeplan.com, meadowmemorials.com.

**AI occasion-poem generators (rising competitive pressure):** aipoemgenerator.co (generate a bespoke funeral/wedding poem).

**Copyright / reprint (PARTIAL, fragmented):** funeralverses.com ("a collection of funeral poems that are in the public domain or free to use"), messengerstationery.com "Poems, Printing, and Permission" (awareness blog), poets.org & poetrysoup.com public-domain collections (general, not occasion-scoped, no per-poem reprint-status tool).

## Read of the niche

The bare **"list of poems for an occasion"** is one of the most saturated content spaces on the web (endless "10 best funeral poems" listicles + relationship/occasion/style finders), and AI generators now also serve "make me one." So a bare finder dies on gate 4, and searching a famous poem by its first line ("I am not there", "I did not die") dies on gate 1 (Google's snippet returns the poem + author instantly).

The thin cuts are all **not-the-poem-text** angles: (1) **reprint/copyright status** — families and program printers infringe by reprinting still-copyrighted poems (Auden's "Funeral Blues", Frye's "Do Not Stand at My Grave"); nobody offers a per-poem PD-vs-copyright flag with safe public-domain swaps; (2) **persona matching** — huge hyper-specific demand ("funeral poem for a knitter / quilter / gardener / pilot / navy veteran") that relationship-based finders don't index by hobby/personality; (3) **delivery** — reading-time, large-print lectern view, pronunciation of archaic words, emotional-difficulty flags for reading aloud. Unique curated metadata is the moat.
