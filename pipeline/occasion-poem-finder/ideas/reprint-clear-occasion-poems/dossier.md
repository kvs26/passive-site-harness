# Dossier: Reprint-clear occasion poems

**A per-poem "safe to print on a program/card?" copyright-status directory with public-domain safe-swaps — rejected at medium confidence: the demand and pain are real, but the idea's stated moat (per-poem PD-vs-copyright flag + safe-swap, "no incumbent does this") is disproven by funeralverses.com, a maintained © 2026 incumbent already ranking on these queries that tags every poem's public-domain status, withholds copyrighted texts (Funeral Blues, The Dash) on dedicated pages, and recommends public-domain alternatives — the exact hook, on the exact flagship poems this idea named.**

## Signal scorecard

**Demand: strong** — the "can I legally print this poem on the order of service / bulletin?" problem recurs across independent communities, help sites and years, and shows up in autocomplete.
  - Alzheimer's Society forum, "Funeral order of service - copyright issues?" — a bereaved family asking exactly this: "Dad passed away last weekend and we are now organising the details of the funeral. Does anyone have a feel for whether there are copyright issues if we want to include some song lyrics or a poem in the order of service booklet?" https://forum.alzheimers.org.uk/threads/funeral-order-of-service-copyright-issues.80165/
  - JustAnswer, "Printing The Dash Poem in Funeral Bulletins: Copyright Q&A" — "Printing a copyrighted poem like 'The Dash' requires permission from the copyright holder unless it is in the public domain" (Bing SERP snippet, this run) https://www.justanswer.com/intellectual-property-law/6yhjo-print-poem-the-dash-copyright-author-linda.html ; second JustAnswer thread, "If I want to adapt (slightly revise) a copyrighted poem from a website [for funeral programs]" (Bing SERP snippet, this run).
  - Elegant Memorials blog, "Understanding Copyright" — "Over the years, customers have asked about using copyrighted quotes or poems in funeral [programs]" (Bing SERP snippet, this run) https://elegantmemorials.com/component/easyblog/?view=entry&id=17 ; plus autocomplete (Google suggest, gl=us, this run): "do not stand at my grave and weep copyright", "public domain funeral poems", "public domain funeral songs", "are rumi poems in the public domain".
  - Note: the *head-term breadth* is thin — plain-mode autocomplete on "funeral poem copyright", "wedding poem copyright", "can you print a copyrighted poem", "public domain poems for funeral" returned essentially nothing this run; demand rests on the pain-source recurrence above, not on a wide query surface.

**SERP & competitor weakness: weak** — the hook is occupied by a fresh, purpose-built incumbent, not by beatable listicles.
  - funeralverses.com (© 2026 Known By One, LLC) collection page states the exact value proposition: "every full text here is in the public domain, so you can print it in a program, read it from the lectern, or adapt it freely without asking anyone's permission," with a "Common questions" entry "Can I print these funeral poems in an order of service?" https://funeralverses.com/funeral-poems/
  - Every poem on that page carries an explicit status label under its full text — "Public domain" / "Traditional — public domain" (Henry Scott Holland "Death Is Nothing at All", Rossetti "Remember", Tennyson "Crossing the Bar", Leigh Hunt "Abou Ben Adhem", An Irish Blessing, Rossetti "Song"). This is the per-poem flag the idea proposed to build.

**Hook depth: strong (but occupied)** — the differentiator genuinely matters to users (real reprint-permission anxiety, above), so the hook is validated as wanted — which is precisely why an incumbent has already built it.
  - funeralverses.com serves the safe-swap — the idea's headline moat — verbatim: for W. H. Auden's still-copyrighted "Funeral Blues", "If you need a poem of comparable weight that you can print freely, the public-domain options in our collection include full texts cleared for any order of service," pairing it with the public-domain "Do Not Stand at My Grave" https://funeralverses.com/poems/funeral-blues-stop-all-the-clocks/
  - For Linda Ellis's "The Dash": "it remains in copyright, so we've given it its own page explaining what it says, why families love it, and public-domain alternatives that carry the same message." The evidence shows the hook matters *and* is answered.

**Monetization paths: weak** — no CSV and no CPC read this run; the one purpose-built incumbent runs an ads-free model, weakening the display-ad thesis for this exact cut.
  - funeralverses.com is explicitly "Free, no sign-ups" with no ad units observed and a free eulogy-builder as its engagement hook (funeral-poems page + Funeral Blues page, this run) — so the plausible route would be memorial-stationery / print-service affiliate rather than AdSense, but no bid data or affiliate tag was fetched this run. Graded weak; not upgradeable here because the idea is being rejected on the hook, not parked on data.

**Feasibility & maintenance: strong** — a static curated poem × PD-status dataset with near-annual refresh; the underlying facts are openly enumerable.
  - Public-domain status is a near-static fact (US pre-1929 cutoff advancing one year each Jan 1) and the reprint-clear canon is small and already enumerated in public (funeralverses' PD collection lists ~a dozen with sources; the copyright cases are the well-known handful — Auden, Frye/Harner, Linda Ellis). No backend, no per-request compute. Feasibility was never the problem.

**Unique-data opportunity: weak** — the "copyright-status metadata" the idea named as its moat is not unique; a maintained incumbent already publishes it and it is derivable public fact.
  - funeralverses.com already carries per-poem status labels, the copyright-vs-PD verdict, the safe-swap, and even the licensing-route detail ("for reprinting it in an order of service, permission should be sought from the Auden estate via the publisher (Curtis Brown represents the estate)") — https://funeralverses.com/poems/funeral-blues-stop-all-the-clocks/ — so there is no defensible data gap left to own.
